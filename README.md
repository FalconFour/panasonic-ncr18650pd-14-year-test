# A Study in Lithium Battery Longevity
A once-in-a-lifetime opportunity struck me, though admittedly about 38 minutes late. "I could be capturing this data...". I know the back story, I know the pack, and I have one more shot (one out of 8 banks) to capture this unique opportunity for the world.

I stuffed an SD card in my iCharger X12 and set it back to work. This is the result.

This is the back story:
The pack was built for a racing motorcycle around 2012. During the race, the pack was damaged by heat of the motor. It was removed and replaced with a backup pack during the event, shipped back to the US with a shipping tag dated July 2012. This pack sat in a crate/pallet, surrounded by packaging material, for nearly 10 years following that. The owner passed it along to me - "can you do something with this?" in effect - and certainly I could.

It was built as a high-voltage, high-discharge pack. 8 banks of 10-series cell groups - 288 volts total. I disconnected the series connections and started testing each group around mid to late 2020. 4 of the banks were totally fine and entered service as a grid-arbitrage battery system: charging during the cheap day hours, discharging during expensive peaks. I found that 4 of the 8 banks had a damaged cell group with significantly worse capacity than the rest. To bring those banks into service, I bypassed the 10th cell group to create "9S" banks.

Three of those banks were recovered that way - the 4th bank was too difficult to access to bypass. Thus, the 4th bank (or 8th bank, depending on perspective) lie dormant for the entire 5 years I used this battery pack in a daily cycle.

Today (June 2026), I finally worked to bring that 4th bank online, and - for the first time in 14 years - charge it.

The system had been found at rest, monitoring voltages for the first time, in this state:
* Cell 1: 3.235v
* Cell 2: 3.233v
* Cell 3: 3.223v
* Cell 4: 3.233v
* Cell 5: 3.210v
* Cell 6: 3.202v
* Cell 7: 3.153v
* Cell 8: 3.125v
* Cell 9: 2.997v

After 14 years out-of-circuit, this is how the pack was found.

After about 38 minutes of charging at 15 amps (approximately 0.25c), the pack leveled out to near-perfect balance. That's when I realized I should be logging this, and stuffed an SD card into the iCharger X12.

The data that follows is the log of the first charging session on this battery pack, followed by a regenerative discharge cycle (boosting regenerative discharge back into my modified Delta Pro's 48-volt pack).
